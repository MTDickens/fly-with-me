# Geographic data and calculation notes

## World map

`src/world-map-data.js` contains simplified Natural Earth 1:110m land polygons, distributed through [world-atlas 2.0.2](https://github.com/topojson/world-atlas). Source: [land-110m.json](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/world-atlas@2.0.2/land-110m.json), Natural Earth 4.1.0. TopoJSON arcs were decoded into longitude/latitude rings and rounded to 0.001°. Natural Earth data are [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/).

The coastline is deliberately coarse. The map uses a rectangular longitude/latitude projection, supports the poles and dateline, and loads no external tiles. It selects a climate location, not real terrain or a precise land-cover survey.

## Historical daily weather

Weather data by [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/), using Copernicus ERA5 reanalysis, under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The panel displays the actual selected calendar day's reanalysis values, not multi-year normals, station observations or a forecast. API details: [Historical Weather API](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api).

`src/climate-default.js` includes only Beijing's **2004-08-14 through 2004-08-28** daily records, retrieved 2026-09-08. Requested coordinates: 39.9° N, 116.4° E; returned grid coordinates: 40.0° N, 116.5° E; elevation: 49 m; timezone: Asia/Shanghai. The opening date is 2004-08-21 (minimum 21.3 °C, maximum 27.8 °C, mean 24.1 °C, precipitation 0.1 mm). Grid data do not imply street-level precision.

To regenerate, request `climateURL(latitude, longitude, weatherWindow(date, radius))` from `src/climate.js` and pass the JSON and the same window to `parseClimate`. The request uses daily minimum, maximum and mean 2 m temperature, precipitation, and FAO reference evapotranspiration, with `models=era5`, `timezone=auto` and `cell_selection=nearest`. A version-2 profile retains start/end dates and one row per actual date: `[low, high, mean, precipitation, ET0]` in °C and mm/day. February 29 uses its own record. Invalid/missing values, gaps, mismatched dates and unexpected units are rejected; another year's data are never substituted as historical weather. Daily weather follows the location's local calendar day even when the display clock is set to UTC.

The default request is ±7 days, **15 inclusive days**. Options are 0, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days either side, so a request can never exceed 61 consecutive days. The available ERA5 archive is guarded at 1940-01-01 and approximately five days before the current date. Edge windows are clipped. The cache keeps one bounded window at each of eight recent coordinates; overlapping dates are reused, and older normals-format storage is discarded. A larger range applies on the next cache miss; reducing it immediately trims stored windows. An in-flight date request is allowed to finish, stale location responses are ignored, requests are separated by at least five seconds, and HTTP 429 honors `Retry-After` with at least a minute of cooldown. There is no continuous retry loop for a stationary failed date. [Open-Meteo quotas](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing) weight long requests: 15 days is a small request, not a guarantee of exactly one quota unit.

A short weather window cannot measure annual climate or identify actual vegetation. The procedural species/density estimate uses a locally generated annual latitude curve, calibrated by the first available window's temperature anomaly and a weak rainfall adjustment (water multiplier 0.8–1.4), plus estimated evapotranspiration. That estimate stays fixed as dates change at the same location; selected-day weather affects foliage, grass dryness and the snow line. No annual precipitation or interannual standard deviation is claimed in the UI. Without a cached daily record, selected-day weather is visibly labeled Estimated. The sun still uses astronomical calculations, independently of weather availability.

## Sun and calendar

`src/astronomy.js` implements Julian-century solar coordinates and equation of time following the Meeus/NOAA approach. Rise/set searches use solar-center altitude −0.833° for a standard unobstructed horizon, including solar radius and conventional refraction. See [NOAA calculation details](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html). Actual observed times depend on terrain, elevation, atmospheric refraction and weather. Civil dates and daylight-saving offsets use the browser's IANA timezone database; unresolved location timezones use explicitly labeled UTC.

`tests/solar-reference.json` contains independent reference calculations from [Astronomy Engine 2.1.19](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy) (MIT), a development-only reference. The selected 16 fixtures cover both hemispheres, polar regions, DST and dates from 1901–2099. Season boundaries use the sun's apparent ecliptic longitude and switch hemisphere south of the equator. The displayed moon and procedural star field remain artistic.
